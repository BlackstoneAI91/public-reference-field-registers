## Why a photo needs its own record

When you compare dog accessories online, the picture and the variant row are two separate pieces of information that a listing presents side by side. The photo shows something; the variant selector names something. Your note should keep them apart so that later, when you are deciding what to order, you can tell whether the picture actually belongs to the colour, size or style you picked.

This reference is written for a fictional Malaysian household working through third-party listings, and it is a note-taking method rather than a verdict on any seller. That matters here: the publication is not the merchant, so the evidence you record should point at the seller's own listing, caption and variant label, not at a summary page.

A useful image note answers four questions. Which version of the listing did I see? What did the seller's caption say the picture shows? Which variant had I selected? And how strong is the link between the picture and that variant? Keep those four answers in fixed columns and the rest of the comparison becomes much easier to audit.

## The four fields, defined narrowly

Listing revision. A listing can change between visits. Record a revision marker you can reproduce: the date and time you checked, plus any visible revision cue such as a stated update date or a changed photo order. Do not call it a version number unless the seller actually shows one.

Seller caption. Copy the caption or alt-style description exactly as the seller wrote it, in the seller's words. If there is no caption, write "no caption shown" rather than describing the image yourself. Your own description belongs in a separate notes field, clearly marked as yours.

Selected variant. Record the variant you had chosen in the selector at the moment the photo was displayed: colour, size, style or pack, using the seller's own labels. If the selector was on a default or no variant was chosen, say so.

Evidence status. Use a small, closed set of labels so the column stays comparable. Suggested labels, as an author heuristic rather than a standard: labelled match (the caption or an on-image label names the same variant you selected); unlabelled (the photo is shown but nothing ties it to your variant); conflicting (the caption or on-image label names a different variant); and not shown (no photo for that variant).

Two rules keep the table honest. First, an image is image evidence only; it does not confirm what will be dispatched. Second, the seller's written confirmation, such as a message stating the variant, is a different kind of evidence and gets its own note, not a promotion of the image status.

## Worked entry: a harness in two colours

Here is a complete fictional entry for a household comparing a dog harness online. The household is imaginary; the structure is what you copy.

Listing revision: checked 14 March, 9:40 pm; photo order changed since the 9 March visit.
Seller caption: "Padded harness, front view" (copied exactly).
Selected variant: colour "Sand", size "Medium", using the seller's labels.
Photo shown: one front-view image of a harness; no colour name appears in the caption or on the image.
Evidence status: unlabelled.
My note (mine, not the seller's): the harness in the photo looks lighter than the "Sand" swatch in the selector, but I cannot tell from the picture alone which colour it is.
Written confirmation: none yet; no message from the seller about the Sand variant.

Read the entry as a whole. The photo exists, the variant was selected, but nothing in the caption or on the image ties the two together. That is why the status is unlabelled rather than labelled match. The household has recorded a real observation without turning it into a claim about the product.

If the same household later wants to compare other accessories, the same four fields carry over. A dog accessories online store listing for a collar, a lead or a coat can be logged in the identical table; only the variant labels change.

## Reclassifying a notice when the photo has no variant label

Reclassification is the part most notes get wrong. Suppose the household first wrote the entry above as labelled match, because the photo appeared immediately after choosing "Sand" and it felt connected. On review, the caption says only "Padded harness, front view" and no on-image label names a colour. The connection was an assumption, not a label.

The correction is a downgrade, not a deletion. Change the status from labelled match to unlabelled, keep the original entry visible with a short note such as "reclassified 15 March: no variant label in caption or image", and leave the listing revision and caption untouched. Downgrading preserves the history and shows exactly which fact changed.

A second case: the seller replies in writing that the Sand variant is the one pictured. That written confirmation is recorded in its own field. It does not silently convert the image status to labelled match, because the image itself still carries no label. You may add a separate line such as "seller written confirmation received 16 March: states Sand is pictured", and treat the two records as distinct.

A third case: a later revision adds an on-image colour tag reading "Sand". Now the image itself carries a label matching your selected variant, so a new entry can be logged as labelled match, with the new revision marker. The old unlabelled entry stays as history.

## Blank table to copy

Copy this into a plain document and add one row per photo per visit. Keep the columns in this order so entries stay comparable across listings.

| Listing revision | Seller caption (exact) | Selected variant (seller labels) | Photo shown | Evidence status | My note (marked as mine) | Seller written confirmation | Reclassified? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (revision) | (exact text) | (variant) | (photo) | (status) | (note) | (reply or blank) | (date or blank) |

Suggested status values: labelled match, unlabelled, conflicting, not shown. Suggested revision marker: date, time and any visible update cue. Suggested reclassification note: what changed, when, and which field it affected.

A few habits make the table more useful. Log the caption before you look closely at the image, so your own impression does not overwrite the seller's words. Record the variant labels exactly, including spelling, because a selector that says "Charcoal" is not the same as one that says "Black". When a listing shows several photos, give each its own row rather than one combined row, since one photo may be labelled and another not. And when you move on to a different category, such as outdoor dog accessories, start a fresh block with its own revision markers rather than mixing categories in one table.

## Image evidence versus written confirmation

The distinction is simple but easy to blur. Image evidence is what the picture and its caption or on-image label show. Written confirmation is a statement from the seller, in text, about a specific variant. Neither one is the same as the product itself arriving in a particular form.

Treat them as parallel records. An image can be a labelled match while written confirmation is absent; a seller can confirm a variant in writing while the photo remains unlabelled. When the two disagree, note the disagreement rather than picking a winner, and keep both entries with their dates.

This is also where the publication's own limits belong. Your table sits in the middle step, recording what the offer displayed.

Commercial details such as price, stock, delivery and returns belong to the live seller listing and its terms, so record them from the same visit as the photo note. If you are comparing costs across offers, keep the currency and pack or variant details alongside the amount so the figures stay like-for-like.

## A short checklist before you rely on a note

Run these checks on any entry you plan to use when deciding what to order. They are author heuristics, not a certified method.

Is the listing revision reproducible, with a date and time? Is the caption copied exactly, or marked as absent? Are the variant labels the seller's own words? Does the evidence status follow from the caption and image alone, without your assumption? Is any written confirmation kept in its own field with its date? If a status changed, is the earlier entry still visible with a reclassification note?

If any answer is no, the entry is a draft, not a record. Fix the field rather than the conclusion.

Finally, keep the scope tight. This reference is about recording which photos belong to a variant. It is not a way to judge whether an accessory suits a particular dog, and it is not health or training advice. For questions about fit, materials or intended use, check the seller's own product information and, where relevant, ask the seller directly in writing, then log the reply in the confirmation field.
